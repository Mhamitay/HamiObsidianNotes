# One-Month Muscle, Strength & Mobility Plan (Day by Day)

> Not medical advice. Given your A1C history and fatty liver, please review this plan with your doctor before starting, especially the fasting ritual and training intensity.

## The Idea
Last cycle you lost fat but also lost muscle. This time the goal is recomposition: build and keep muscle while improving A1C and body composition, using lifting plus enough protein instead of relying mainly on cardio and juice.

## Daily Nutrition Template
Applies every day unless noted otherwise.

1. On waking, empty stomach: 1 teaspoon olive oil, apple cider vinegar, cayenne pepper, and lemon in water.
2. About 2 hours later, green juice: kale, cucumber, celery, a small green apple, lemon, and ginger.
3. First solid meal, add a protein anchor: eggs, Greek yogurt, or a protein shake alongside the juice. This is the key fix compared to last time.
4. Lunch: lean protein, non-starchy vegetables, and a healthy fat. No bread, pasta, rice, or potato.
5. Dinner: meat, fish, or eggs plus vegetables. Avoid heavy carbs late in the day.
6. Evening craving fix: protein powder and yogurt mix.
7. On training days: a small protein snack before lifting if hungry, and protein again within 1 to 2 hours after training.

## Finding Your Starting Weight (Do This Before Day 1)

You will not know your right weight until you test it. For each of the main lifts below, use this simple process on your first attempt:

1. Pick a light-to-moderate weight as a guess.
2. Do 8 to 10 reps with good form.
3. Ask yourself: could I have done 3 or more additional reps with that same weight?
    - If yes, it was too light. Increase the weight next set.
    - If you could barely finish the last 1 to 2 reps with good form, and any more would break your form, that is your working weight. Use it going forward.
    - If you could not complete 8 reps with good form, it was too heavy. Decrease the weight next set.
4. Repeat this test set by set until you land on a weight where reps 8 through 10 are hard but clean. That becomes your starting number for that lift in Week 1.

### Quick Reference by Lift

- **Goblet Squat / Dumbbell Romanian Deadlift:** Most beginners land somewhere in the 15 to 30 lb dumbbell range per hand (or one dumbbell held at chest for goblet squat), but body type and prior training matter, so let the rep test decide, not this number.
- **Dumbbell Bench Press / Seated Shoulder Press:** Often lighter than leg lifts, many beginners start around 10 to 20 lb per dumbbell.
- **Lat Pulldown / Seated Cable Row:** Machine weight stacks vary a lot by gym, so ignore the plate number and just use the rep test.
- **Bicep Curl / Triceps Pushdown / Face Pull:** These are usually your lightest lifts, often 5 to 15 lb range to start.
- **Walking Lunges, Calf Raises, Push-Ups, Plank, Hanging Knee Raise:** Start with just bodyweight (no added weight) until form is solid, then add dumbbells later if needed.

## Guardrails Against Last Time's Problem

1. Never train fasted on lifting days without at least the protein-containing breakfast. Juice alone before lifting risks muscle loss.
2. If the scale drops but strength also drops, that is the warning sign. It means you are losing muscle, not just fat. Add more protein and or reduce cardio slightly rather than cutting more food.
3. Track one non-scale metric, such as a lift number or a photo, alongside the scale, so progress is not judged by weight alone.

## Weekly Tracking Checklist

- Body weight, same day and time each week
- One or two lift numbers, such as squat and bench weight used
- How clothes fit
- Energy levels
- Next A1C check with your doctor. This plan supports it, but the lab result is what matters most.
